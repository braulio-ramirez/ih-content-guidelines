# Glossary

Source: Included Health Terms and Healthcare Terms (Confluence, PD space), read directly. Decisions from the Tier 3 review are applied and marked **(Decision)**. Feature-name rules are in [terminology.md](terminology.md). Grammar rules are in [grammar.md](grammar.md). Inclusive and community terms are in [inclusive-language.md](inclusive-language.md).

## Care Team and people

- **Care Team.** Always capitalize and spell out in member-facing copy. Use "the Care Team" in general and "your Care Team" when it is personal. Use "Care Team" when referring to more than one advocate. Do: "Care Team." Don't: "care team."
- **Member.** Preferred over "user" or "patient" in member-facing copy. Do: "Included Health members have access to a wide variety of benefits at no cost to them."
- **Member Care Advocate (MCA).** **(Decision)** Care Coordinator is deprecated. Members now have Member Care Advocates (MCAs). Use "MCA" only in the Messages section of the app, when a member is chatting with an MCA. Everywhere else, say "advocate" or "member of the Care Team."
- **Provider.** **(Decision)** Use for a general pool of clinicians. This is also the accepted term for any member of the clinical team, regardless of title.
- **Doctor.** **(Decision)** Use only for an individual provider who holds an MD or DO. Do not use "doctor" for a general pool; use "provider." Include "doctor" in CTAs only when it names one provider.
- **Clinician, physician.** Avoid in member-facing copy. For a group, use "Care Team."
- **Patient.** Avoid, except in active or ongoing care.
- **Nurse practitioner (NP), physician assistant (PA), registered nurse (RN).** Use only for an individual provider who holds that credential. Spell out on first mention, then use the abbreviation. "Nurse" is acceptable as a short form for NP and RN.
- **Primary care provider (PCP).** Spell out on first mention, then use "PCP."
- **Specialist.** Say "specialist." Name the type of specialist when it helps the member.
- **Therapist.** In mental health flows, "therapist" needs no further label. For other types, add context, such as "physical therapist."
- **Psychiatrist.** A medical doctor who can prescribe. Use only for an individual provider who is a practicing psychiatrist.
- **Psychologist.** Not a medical doctor and cannot prescribe. Use rarely, and only for an individual provider.
- **Client, customer.** Avoid in member-facing copy. Both are internal terms for the employers who offer Included Health.
- **Hub, Case.** Internal only. Do not use "Hub" in member-facing messaging. Use "case" sparingly and only for actions the Care Team takes behind the scenes. For a member's action, describe it instead: "Your claims support request has been submitted," not "Your case has been submitted."

## Included Health services and programs

- **All-Included Care™.** Always include the ™ symbol when you name this package.
- **Nurse line.** **(Decision)** The telephone service where members call and talk to a nurse about being sick. The nurse advises whether to go to the ER, try home remedies, and more. Use "nurse line" for the service. Do not use "triage" in member-facing copy; it is medical jargon. Do: "Call the nurse line for quick advice on symptoms, side effects, and more."
- **Nurse.** **(Decision)** Allowed in member-facing copy. This includes "nurse line" and "Nurse Care Manager" (CCM).
- **Care and Case Management (CCM).** You can name the program, but describe the benefit so members understand what it gives them. Many CCM members get a Nurse Care Manager, so "nurse" and "Nurse Care Manager" are allowed here.
- **Care History.** Use for the app's section that lists a member's past care. Do not use "Claims History." Capitalize when it names the section.
- **Primary Care Kit.** Capitalize; it is an official product name. Do: "Your Primary Care Kit."
- **Virtual primary care (VPC).** Spell out in member-facing messaging. Use lowercase unless it starts a sentence. Do: "Choose virtual primary care." Don't: "Choose VPC."
- **Urgent care.** To distinguish virtual urgent care from primary care, use "on-demand video visit" or "see a doctor now." Do: "Schedule an on-demand video visit to see a doctor now." Don't: "Schedule a virtual urgent care appointment."
- **Community services: Black Health and LGBTQ+ Health.** Use the official names for these services. Use neutral wording for the communities, as in [inclusive-language.md](inclusive-language.md). Use "Black health" only for the service name.

## Visits

- **Visit.** Prefer over "appointment." Both are acceptable, but "visit" is shorter and plainer.
- **Video visit, phone visit.** Prefer these when you know the format. Use "virtual visit" only when the format is unknown. Do not use "online visit" or "remote visit."
- **Telehealth.** Use only when a member is searching for external providers in Provider Connect and needs to know if they offer virtual services. Do not use "telemedicine."
- **Follow-up visit.** Use instead of "routine visit."
- **Annual check-up.** **(Decision)** Always use this term for the once-a-year preventive visit that every member gets covered at no cost through their insurance. This is the visit the source calls the annual wellbeing visit. Do not use "annual wellbeing visit." (Decided by Jourdyn Lasalvia and Kate Gulrajani.)

## Costs and coverage

- **Copay.** One word, no hyphen. Denote as a dollar amount (with the $ sign). Do not use "co-pay."
- **Coinsurance.** One word, no hyphen. Denote as a percentage (with the % sign).
- **Deductible.** Denote as a dollar amount.
- **Out-of-pocket maximum.** Hyphenate as an adjective: "out-of-pocket maximum." Use "out-of-pocket max" when space is tight. Do not hyphenate the prepositional phrase: "You'll pay $300 out of pocket."
- **No-cost, no cost.** Use "no-cost" before a noun: "a no-cost benefit." Use "no cost" after a preposition: "at no cost to you."
- **In-network, out-of-network.** Hyphenate both.
- **Coverage.** No need for "insurance" before it. Do: "your coverage." Don't: "your insurance coverage."
- **Bill, claim.** Use "bill" only when the member owes money. Use "claim" otherwise.
- **Claims denial.** Use the full phrase. "Denial" alone can be read as a service denial. Explain why the claim was denied.
- **Appeal.** Fine to use.
- **Reconsideration.** Avoid.
- **Overpayment.** Avoid. Explain that the member was charged based on the information available at the time, which was a system issue, not something the member did.
- **Balance billing.** Avoid as a term. Explain the concept instead.
- **Billing provider.** Use the full phrase. Do not shorten it to "provider."
- **Coding review.** Avoid the term. Explain the outcome and what to expect.
- **Allowed amount.** Define it when you use it. It is the maximum an insurer will pay for a covered service.
- **Prepayment.** Say that it is based on an estimate and the final amount may differ after the claim is processed.
- **Preventive and diagnostic.** When explaining coverage, say which one the service is. Preventive care is often covered at no cost. Diagnostic care may have a copay or coinsurance.
- **Explanation of Benefits (EOB), Flexible Spending Account (FSA), Health Savings Account (HSA).** Spell out and capitalize on first mention on each screen, then use the acronym.
- **Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes.** "CPT codes" is the preferred form.
- **Cost estimate.** Use instead of "cost transparency."

## Mental health

- **Mental health.** Use in member-facing copy. Do not use "behavioral health" or "BH." **(Decision)** "Behavioral health" is fine internally.
- **Coaching.** Use "chat-based coaching" when space allows. Which phrase to use is tracked in [FOLLOWUPS.md](../../FOLLOWUPS.md).
- **Condition.** Use "condition," not "disorder."
- **Anxiety.** Plain alternatives: "worry," "fear," "nervousness," "unease."
- **Depression.** Plain alternatives: "sadness," "sorrow," "grief," "gloom."
- **Therapy.** In mental health flows, no need to add "mental health." For other therapy, add context: "physical therapy."
- **Psychiatry and psychology.** Do not confuse them. Psychiatrists are medical doctors. Psychologists are not.
- **Substance use disorder (SUD).** See [inclusive-language.md](inclusive-language.md).

## Plain language for medical terms

| Medical term | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| Antibiotics | Drugs |
| Bacteria | Germs (use the medical term only when explaining the difference from viruses) |
| Carcinogen | "May cause cancer" |
| Carcinoma | "A type of cancer" (define it if you must use the term) |
| Diagnosis | "To learn" or "to figure out" the cause |
| Disease | Sickness, illness, health problem |
| Epidemic | Widespread illness |
| Glucose | Blood sugar |
| Hyperglycemia, hypoglycemia | High blood sugar, low blood sugar |
| Hypertension, hypotension | High blood pressure, low blood pressure |
| Immunization | Getting or giving a vaccine or shot |
| Imaging | X-ray, CT scan, MRI, or ultrasound when you know which; "imaging" when you don't |
| Infection | Illness, sickness (use "bacterial" or "viral" only when explaining the difference) |
| Labs, as a synonym for lab tests or results | Lab test, lab results. Do not use "labs" alone. |
| Musculoskeletal (MSK) | Spell out on first use with "muscle, bone, and joint." Then use "MSK." |
| Preventative | Preventive |
| Respiration | Breathing, inhaling |
| Respiratory system | Name the parts: lungs, nose, throat, windpipe |
| Triage | Do not use. Say "nurse line" for the service. |
| Vaccine | Shot, medicine, protection against a disease |
| Virus | Germ (use the medical term only when explaining the difference from bacteria) |
| Dose / dosage | Be clear about the amount, how many doses, and over what period |
| Healthcare | One word |
