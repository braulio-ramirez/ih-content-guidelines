# Grammar and mechanics

Source: Grammar and Mechanics pages (Confluence, PD space). Rules are restated from those pages with their examples. Calendar and date layout for components is in [components/calendar.md](components/calendar.md). Dashes, apostrophes, colons, and other UI punctuation are in [ux-copy.md](ux-copy.md) (Punctuation in UI copy) where they apply to UI.

## Abbreviations and acronyms

- Spell out an abbreviation or acronym the first time, unless members will recognize it. Use the short form after that. Example: "Ear Nose and Throat (ENT) doctor," then "ENT doctor."
- Days and months: 3 letters, no periods. `Sep`, `Thu`. Not `Sept.` or `Thur.`
- Time zones: abbreviate, no periods (`PST`, `PDT`). See Dates and time.
- Healthcare titles and terms: use the approved forms on the Abbreviations & Acronyms page (for example `MD`, `DO`, `NP`, `PA-C`, `RN`, `MRI`, `EKG`).
- Units: follow AP Style, including for pounds.

## Numbers

- Spell out 0–9 in running text: "three tiers," not "3 tiers."
- Use numerals for timestamps (`5:02 PM`), series and ranges (`5 of 100 results`), currency (`$5`), units (follow AP Style), and lists.
- Use numerals for 10 and up: `10`, `500`, `10,545`.
- Abbreviate large round numbers only: `10 million`, `2.5 million`. Do not abbreviate unrounded numbers.
- Use `%`, not the word "percent": `75%`.
- Spell out ordinals: `first, second, third`. Not `1st, 2nd, 3rd`.
- Decimals: use a period and numerals with a leading zero (`0.25`). Use no more than two decimal places.
- Fractions: spell them out (`one fourth`). Hyphenate them as adjectives (`a two-thirds majority vote`). Do not hyphenate them as nouns (`one third of the providers`).
- Phone numbers: `1-800-123-4567`.

## Dates and time

- Weekdays: capitalize. Use the full name on first exposure. Abbreviate to 3 letters, no periods, in compact forms (`Thu`). Add a comma after the weekday when a date follows: `Friday, August 7, 2026` or `Fri, Aug 7, 2026`.
- Days: numerals only, no ordinal suffixes, no leading zeros. `Apr 3`, not `Apr 3rd` or `Apr 03`.
- Months: capitalize. Use the full name on first exposure, and 3 letters with no period after that (`Nov`, not `Nov.`). For a compact numeric form, drop the leading zero: `2/11`, not `02/11`.
- Years: 4 digits (`2024`, not `24` or `'24`). Once the year has appeared on a screen, don't repeat it in later date references.
- AM and PM: uppercase, no periods, one space before the meridian: `12:00 PM`. This departs from AP Style at Engineering's request.
- Time ranges: if the whole range falls before or after 12:00 PM, use AM or PM once: `6:30–11:00 AM`. If the range spans 12:00 PM, include both: `9:00 AM–5:00 PM`. Use an en dash with no spaces.
- Timestamps: add a comma after the date, and omit "at" before the time: `Fri, Aug 14, 10:30 AM`.
- Top of the hour includes `:00`: `5:00 PM`, not `5 PM`.
- Time zones: abbreviate, no periods, one space before the zone: `3:15 PM PST`. Use `S` for standard time (`PST`) and `D` for daylight time (`PDT`). Do not use `PT`. Daylight time runs from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November. Outside that window, use standard time.
- Relative time: `today`, `tomorrow`, `last week` are allowed. Add the date when space allows: "Your appointment is tomorrow, Apr 15." Use relative time or the weekday, not both: `Tomorrow, April 3, 10:30 AM` or `Friday, April 3, 10:30 AM`. Not `Tomorrow Friday, April 3`.

## Punctuation

- Use the Oxford comma: `bananas, rice, applesauce, and toast`.
- Em dash (—): joins related thoughts. Put a space on each side: `Call the 24/7 care line for treatment advice — at no cost to you.`
- En dash (–): ranges of numbers or time, with no spaces: `Monday–Friday, 5:00 AM–6:00 PM`. If "to" fits in place of the dash, use an en dash. If it doesn't, use an em dash.
- Hyphen (-): compound words, with no spaces: `follow-up`, `sign-up`, `15-minute visit`.
- Ampersand (&): use "and." An ampersand is acceptable only in headers and CTAs when character count is a concern.
- Apostrophe ('): possessives and contractions. Singular nouns get `'s` (`your doctor's instructions`). Singular nouns ending in s take an apostrophe alone (`Texas'`). Plural nouns that end in s take an apostrophe alone: `dependents'`. Plural nouns that do not end in s take `'s`: `children's`. Do not use apostrophes in place of quotation marks.
- Colon (:): introduces a list or steps. Put each item or step on its own line.
- Parentheses: a period goes outside a fragment in parentheses: `wear a mask (surgical, cloth, or KN95).` A period goes inside when the parentheses hold a full standalone sentence, and the surrounding sentence also ends with its own period.
- Question mark (?): goes inside quotation marks only when the question is part of the quote.
- Quotation marks: for words, letters, file names, titles of published works, and direct quotes. For UI labels, use bold, not quotation marks: "go to **Get Care**."
- Exclamation marks: not in UI copy (see [SKILL.md](SKILL.md)).

## Contractions

- Contractions are fine in conversational copy: `you'll`, `we're`, `they've`.
- Spell out negative contractions when the message is that something is not possible. Do: "Your doctor does not have availability this week." Don't: "Your doctor doesn't have availability this week."
- Do not confuse `who's` (who is) with `whose`, and `you're` (you are) with `your`.

## Points of view

- Use second person (`you`, `your`) almost always. It speaks to the member directly.
- Avoid first person about Included Health services. Do: "Get started today." Don't: "Let us help you get started."
- First person (`I`, `me`, `my`) is acceptable in CTAs, because it gives the member ownership: `Activate my account`.

## Verb tense

- Use the simple tense: "You book a visit," not "You have been booking a visit."
- Helping verbs (`has`, `have`, `is`, `are`, `was`, `were`) are a sign you are not in the simple tense. They can also signal passive voice (see [voice.md](voice.md)).

## Capitalization

- Sentence case by default. Capitalize the first word and proper nouns.
- Title case only for page titles and screen titles (see [ux-copy.md](ux-copy.md)).
- Do not use ALL CAPS. It is hard to read, and it can feel like yelling.

## Lists

- If one list item is a full sentence with punctuation, every item gets punctuation. Otherwise, omit punctuation from every item. (Decision: UX Copy rule. The Punctuation page's multi-sentence trigger does not apply. See [ux-copy.md](ux-copy.md).)
